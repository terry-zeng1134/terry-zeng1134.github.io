Rokt's pitch to advertisers is conversion lift. Lift you cannot attribute causally is a claim rather than a measurement — it can't be distinguished from shoppers who were going to convert anyway.

I defined and shipped the incrementality platform that measures whether Rokt's conversion lift is actually causal, and made its output the number advertisers buy against.

Result: $10M in net-new advertiser spend secured.

How I knew it was real: The number was sold against, not just reviewed internally — advertisers committed budget on the strength of it, which is a harder test than an internal sign-off.

Rokt's ads run inside its partners' checkout flows. An ad shown at the wrong moment doesn't just underperform — it can cost the partner the sale it was placed next to.

I shipped the logistic regression model that predicts when a shopper is likely to abandon checkout and suppresses Rokt's own ads in that moment.

Result: $2.5bn in partner revenue loss prevented.

How I knew it was real: Suppression trades Rokt's ad revenue for the partner's checkout revenue, so the threshold is a business decision rather than a model-fit one: it belongs where protected partner revenue exceeds the Rokt revenue given up, not where F1 peaks.

A new ad placement only earns out if partners can actually get it live. Launch-to-live time, not demand, was the binding constraint.

I shipped the experimentation tooling that cut partner launch-to-live time on a new payment-page placement.

Result: placement revenue tripled to $75M.

How I knew it was real: Launch-to-live time was the mechanism and it was directly observable in partner ramp times, so the tooling's effect could be read before it showed up in revenue.

An alpha Upsells product needed to prove it beat the client's existing baseline before it could earn wider rollout.

I ran the A/B program behind it — the test design, the metric definitions, and the read.

Result: a sustained 20% lift in value per transaction over client baseline.

How I knew it was real: The lift was measured against the client's own baseline and held across windows rather than landing in a single test read — the load-bearing word is sustained.

Advertiser budget parked where it doesn't convert is inventory the auction never gets to sell.

I shipped the budget allocation model that predicts where advertiser spend actually converts, freeing the stranded inventory back into the auction.

Result: network revenue per transaction up 2%.

How I knew it was real: A reallocation model can post a lift just by moving spend toward conversions that were already going to happen. The honest test is whether network revenue per transaction rises, not whether the reallocated campaigns look good — which is why that was the metric.

Campaign issues were being diagnosed by hand, and nobody knew which classes of failure the existing detection actually covered.

I shipped an LLM agent that diagnoses campaign issues, then graded its outputs and classified 372 incidents drawn from 11 months of chat logs.

Result: detection coverage redesigned against the failure classes the incident corpus actually contained.

How I knew it was real: The agent's outputs were graded rather than assumed correct, and the 372-incident corpus set the denominator: coverage was measured against failures that had actually occurred, not against a hypothesized failure list.

Embedded chat connecting e-commerce shoppers with product experts. Format, copy and placement decisions were being made without a way to test them.

I owned the shopper funnel and built the company's first experimentation program, which became how the team decided what shipped.

Result: shopper onboarding tripled and usage-fee revenue grew 230%.

How I knew it was real: Before the program there was no counterfactual for a format, copy or placement change. Its first job was making “it worked” a falsifiable claim; the revenue number is what survived that.

Sole data hire at an early-stage company with no analytics function and no one else to build it.

I built the function from zero — a Python, Rockset and SQL query repo — and trained two non-technical reports to serve requests from it themselves.

Result: data-request turnaround cut 90%.

How I knew it was real: The 90% is a turnaround measure, not a volume one. The real test was that two non-technical reports could serve requests without me — the function had to outlive any query I personally wrote.

The product had several plausible conversion levers and no evidence about which of them carried revenue.

I built the regression models that traced revenue to personalized recommendations, expert response time and retargeting, then drove the product roadmap changes those models implied.

Result: purchase conversion up 26%.

How I knew it was real: The models identified candidate levers; the 26% is the measured outcome after the roadmap changes shipped, not the effect size the models predicted.

A consumer-brand venture fund built on an in-house data platform, screening far more candidates than a team could diligence by hand.

I owned quantitative due diligence and built the NLP and sentiment models that narrowed 30 candidates down to 6 investments.

Result: $17M deployed at roughly 3x return.

How I knew it was real: The ~3x is realized, not modeled. The NLP work narrowed the funnel from 30 to 6 — the return is what those 6 investments actually did afterward.

Speed to term sheet is the product in capital-as-a-service — it is what a founder is choosing between lenders on.

I owned performance of the product and partnered with ML engineers to productionize the value signals the underwriting depended on.

Result: term sheets delivered 4x faster than the industry standard.

How I knew it was real: The 4x is against an external industry benchmark rather than a before-and-after against our own prior pace.

A $350M/year gold mine had an operation nobody was measuring, so its losses were invisible and therefore unprioritized.

I built the Python/SQL pipeline that quantified the operation for the first time and ranked its loss drivers by size.

Result: roughly $33M in annual production loss eliminated.

How I knew it was real: The operation had never been measured, so the baseline had to be constructed rather than observed — and the loss drivers were ranked by size before anything was changed, so the sequencing wasn't retrofitted to the result.

Ten declining sales teams, none of them working from a shared definition of what good looked like.

I defined their north-star and leading KPIs in SQL and Tableau, then drove the upsell, cross-sell and churn plays to adoption across all ten.

Result: $25M in annualized revenue added.

How I knew it was real: Ten teams meant each team's change could be read against the other nine rather than only against its own past, which separates the plays that worked from a market-wide move.
