Senior Data Scientist, Product · Jul 2024 – Present

Rokt sells conversion lift. Lift that can't be separated from conversions which would have happened anyway is a claim rather than a measurement — and an advertiser has no reason to pay for it.

I designed the incrementality experiments that isolate product-driven conversions from baseline, then equipped GTM and cross-functional stakeholders to sell the measured lift.

Result: $10M in net-new advertiser spend won.

How I knew it was real: The experiment defines the baseline the lift is measured against, so isolating product-driven conversions is the argument — and the number then had to survive advertisers deciding whether to commit budget against it, which is a harder test than internal review.

Rokt's ads sit inside its partners' checkout and confirmation flows. An advertiser that raises a shopper's churn risk can cost the partner far more than that ad placement earns.

I productionized the logistic regression model that predicts shopper churn and reorders the auction to prioritize advertisers who minimize that risk.

Result: $2.5B in partner revenue loss prevented.

How I knew it was real: Reordering trades auction yield against partner churn, so the ranking function has to price churn risk in the same units as bid value. That calibration is a business decision, not a model-fit one — the model can be well-calibrated and the ranking still wrong.

Static bids leave advertiser budget stranded on campaigns that could absorb more spend efficiently, and overspend where they can't.

I led the 0-to-1 launch of conversion elastic bidding, adjusting bids dynamically to maximize cost-per-conversion efficiency, and measured the impact with difference-in-differences.

Result: +15% spend on campaigns.

How I knew it was real: A bidding change is hard to A/B cleanly, because treated and control campaigns compete in the same auction and treatment leaks across the boundary. Difference-in-differences was the identification strategy chosen to handle that, rather than a split test that would have quietly measured the wrong thing.

Campaign-delivery failures were diagnosed by hand, and advertiser spend sat blocked for as long as the tracing took.

I shipped an AI agent that diagnoses infrastructure and operational failures in campaign delivery.

Result: resolution cut from 1.5 weeks to 24 hours, unblocking roughly $100M in advertiser spend.

How I knew it was real: Resolution time is the metric that matters here, not diagnostic accuracy in isolation — a correct diagnosis that arrives in a week has already cost the spend it existed to protect.

Senior Data Analyst → Senior Data Scientist · Aug 2022 – Feb 2024

Embedded chat connecting e-commerce shoppers with product experts. Formats, copy and placements shipped on judgment, because there was no mechanism to test them.

I owned funnel onboarding performance and built the company's A/B experimentation program, which became how the team decided what shipped.

Result: shopper onboarding tripled and usage-fee revenue grew 230%.

How I knew it was real: Before the program there was no counterfactual for a format or copy change at all. Its first job was making “it worked” a falsifiable statement; the revenue number is what survived that, not what preceded it.

Analyst, Venture Equity · Nov 2021 – Aug 2022

A consumer-brand fund screening far more candidates than a team could diligence by hand, with the signal buried in unstructured customer language.

I built the PySpark NLP pipeline that TF-IDF vectorized purchase-criteria language from product reviews, narrowing 30 candidates to 6 investments.

Result: $17M deployed at roughly 3x return.

How I knew it was real: The ~3x is realized, not modeled. The pipeline's job ended at narrowing the funnel — the return is what those 6 investments actually did afterward, which is the only version of this number that means anything.
